IKEA AFTONSPARV → ESP32 + WLED

Swap the lamp's IKEA controller for an ESP32-C3 running WLED, powered from one USB charger through the lamp's own cord. You'll get app and Home Assistant control, effects and schedules.

⏱ ~2–3 hours🔌 USB 5 V → boost to 24 V3 × MOSFET PWM

Four rules that protect the parts

Unplug before rewiringPull the USB charger out of the wall before you move any wire.
Never USB + lamp power togetherNever plug the C3's USB-C into your Mac while the lamp cord is powered. The two 5 V supplies would fight each other.
Set 24.0 V before the lampThe MT3608 ships at a random voltage, up to 28 V. Set it with nothing attached to its output.
MOSFET legs: G · D · SWith the printing facing you and the legs down: Gate, Drain, Source, left to right. The metal tab is also Drain.

What you need

ESP32-C3 SuperMini1 of your 2. Runs WLED.
MT3608 boost1 of 5. 5 V in → 24 V out.
IRLZ44N ×3One per colour, plus spares in case of fakes.
Resistors (BOJACK kit)100 Ω: brown-black-brown. 10 kΩ: brown-black-orange.
  • Multimeter + PVC tape (Wickes)
  • Soldering iron, solder, wire strippers/cutters
  • USB-A wall charger, 5 V 2 A or more
  • A spare USB-A cable to cut up
  • Small screwdriver (lamp screws and MT3608 trim screw)
  • Breadboard + jumper wires (Freenove kit) for the test build
  • Mac with Chrome, for flashing WLED
  • Stripboard (optional; otherwise wire point-to-point)

Step by step

1

Open the lamp and photograph it

  1. Unplug the lamp. Lay it face-down on a towel.
  2. Undo the three screws on the base ring. One hides under the sticker; feel for it, then peel the sticker back.
  3. Lift off the bottom cover only. The top doesn't need to come off.
  4. Photograph the original board and its wires before you touch anything.
Measure the free space in the channel (width × depth). You'll need it in step 3.
2

Desolder and label the six wires

  1. Desolder all six wires from the IKEA board. The board goes in the bin.
  2. Wrap a small tape flag on each wire and write its job on it:
WireJobGoes to (later)
LED whiteLED + (common)MT3608 VOUT+ (24 V)
LED blackRedMOSFET 1 drain
LED greenGreenMOSFET 2 drain
LED transparentBlueMOSFET 3 drain
Cord whitePower +5 V rail
Cord blackPower −GND rail
There are two white wires and two black wires. The tape flags are what keep them apart.
3

Check everything will fit

The electronics have to fit in that curved channel. Compare the channel against these sizes:

PartSize (mm)
ESP32-C3 SuperMini18 × 22.5 × ~4 (no header pins)
MT360836 × 17 × 14 (the trim screw is the tall part)
IRLZ44N10 × 15 body, lies 4.5 thick when bent flat
Space savers: solder wires straight to the C3 instead of fitting its header pins. Bend each MOSFET's legs 90° so it lies flat. Spread the parts along the channel rather than stacking them.
If the MT3608 won't fit, stop and ask before cutting anything. The fallback is a small box on the cord.
4

Flash WLED onto the C3 (before any wiring)

  1. Plug the bare C3 into your Mac with a USB-C data cable. Nothing else should be connected to it.
  2. In Chrome, open install.wled.me → Install → pick the C3's serial port → install.
  3. Not detected? Hold BOOT, tap RST, release BOOT, then try again.
  4. When the installer offers Wi-Fi setup, join your home network, then open WLED.
  5. Config → LED Preferences: output 1 = PWM RGB, GPIOs 4 5 6. Save.
  6. Config → WiFi Setup: if there's a TX power option, set it to about 8.5 dBm. SuperMini antennas drop out at full power.
  7. Unplug the C3 from the Mac. From now on it updates over Wi-Fi.
If WLED ever loses your Wi-Fi, it opens its own WLED-AP hotspot. Join it from your phone to fix it, so there's no need to open the lamp.
5

Learn the five C3 pins you'll use

PinConnects to
5V5 V rail (cord white)
G (GND)GND rail (cord black)
4100 Ω → MOSFET 1 gate (red)
5100 Ω → MOSFET 2 gate (green)
6100 Ω → MOSFET 3 gate (blue)
Leave 3.3, 2, 8, 9 and 20/21 free. 2, 8 and 9 affect booting.
6

Turn the IKEA cord into a USB cord

  1. The cord is a detachable lead: it plugs into a DC socket on the lamp. James's lead has a barrel plug on both ends (it unplugs from the adapter too): cut one plug off right behind the plug, and keep the full-length lead with the other plug for the lamp. Bin the adapter: any 24 V barrel adapter would fit the socket and kill the C3, so mark the plug "5 V USB only".
  2. Cut the USB-A cable, keeping the end with the big USB-A plug. Strip it: you'll find red, black, green and white.
  3. Cut green and white short and tape them back. They're data wires and aren't needed.
  4. Find which cord conductor is +. Push the barrel plug into the lamp's socket. Set the meter to continuity (🔊) and touch each cut conductor against the cord-white wire inside the lamp (the plain white on the socket). The one that beeps is +.
  5. Join USB red → cord + and USB black → cord −. Stagger the two joints, solder, and tape each one separately, then tape over both.
  6. Plug into the charger. Inside the lamp, meter cord-white to cord-black: it should read +5 V (a minus sign means swapped). Unplug.
7

Set the MT3608 to exactly 24.0 V

  1. Solder short wires to all four MT3608 pads.
  2. VIN+ → cord-white (+5 V), VIN− → cord-black. Nothing on VOUT yet.
  3. Meter on DC volts, probes on VOUT+ / VOUT−. Plug the charger in.
  4. Turn the brass trim screw clockwise about 20 turns. It clicks at the end and never stops hard. The meter should fall to about 5 V.
  5. Now turn anticlockwise, slowly, until the meter reads 24.0 V.
  6. Unplug. Dab of tape over the screw so it can't get knocked.
The listing says anticlockwise raises the voltage. If yours goes the other way, believe the meter. Never go above 24.5 V.
8

Wire one MOSFET channel (×3)

  1. Build it on the breadboard first, using the Freenove kit's jumpers. Solder only once it works.
  2. Gate (left leg): 100 Ω in series from the GPIO.
  3. 10 kΩ from gate to source, so the LEDs stay off while the C3 boots.
  4. Source (right leg): to the GND rail.
  5. Drain (middle leg): to that colour's lamp wire.
MOSFETGate fromDrain to
1GPIO 4LED black (R)
2GPIO 5LED green (G)
3GPIO 6LED transparent (B)
Updated 2026-10-14: soldering on the bare legs was too cramped. Splay G and S about 30°, put one wire on each leg, fit the 10 kΩ between the gate and source wires about 4 cm away, and put the 100 Ω inline in the gate wire, further along towards the C3. See step8-mosfet-module.html.
9

Connect everything

  1. Charger unplugged. Join the cord-white wire, C3 5V and MT3608 VIN+ (the 5 V rail).
  2. Join the cord-black wire, C3 G, MT3608 VIN−/VOUT− and all three MOSFET sources (the GND rail).
  3. MT3608 VOUT+ → LED white.
  4. Gates and drains as in step 8.
10

Test one colour at a time

  1. Start with only Q1 (red) connected. Plug in the charger.
  2. Open WLED and pick pure red. The lamp should glow red.
  3. Drag brightness from 1% to 100%. Smooth dimming = genuine IRLZ44N. Dim at 100% or dead = a fake, so swap in a spare.
  4. Touch the MOSFET. Warm is fine, too hot to hold is not.
  5. Unplug, add Q2 (green), test. Then Q3 (blue).
  6. Wrong colour showing? Don't rewire: swap the GPIO numbers in LED Preferences.
Voltage check with the lamp on full white:
  • cord-white to cord-black inside the lamp: above 4.5 V is fine (the thin cord loses a little)
  • MT3608 VOUT: still about 24 V
Flickering, or the C3 rebooting at high brightness, means the charger is too weak or the cord drops too much. Try a 2 A+ charger first.
11

Make it permanent and close up

  1. Move the circuit from the breadboard to stripboard, or solder it point-to-point.
  2. Bend the MOSFETs flat. Tape every bare joint and the MT3608's pins.
  3. Lay the parts along the channel so no bare metal touches another part.
  4. Power on once more with the cover off and run a full colour cycle.
  5. Screw the cover back on and refit the sticker.
  6. In WLED: name it, set a default preset, and add it to Home Assistant (auto-discovered).
