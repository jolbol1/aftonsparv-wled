1. Lay it out in the order the power flows: ① power in at one end, ② MT3608, ③ the three switches, then the cable out at the far end. Wires stay short and never double back.
  2. Nodes A and B: the neatest no-solder option is two small Wago 221 lever connectors (a 3-way for A, a 3-way for B). Socket, MT3608 and C3 wires clip in, and so do C1's legs. Pigtails also work, just taped.
  3. C1 sits right beside the MT3608's VIN, with its legs as short as possible.
  4. MOSFETs lie flat in a row, tabs taped. Join the three source wires into one and run it to VOUT−.
  5. The C3 goes in the far corner from the MT3608, with its antenna end against the box wall and as far from the coil as the box allows. Its 5V/G taps run along the wall from A/B, and the gate wires are short hops up to the switches.
  6. Fix everything down with double-sided foam tape or a dab of hot glue, so nothing rattles or shorts when the box moves.
  7. Strain relief on both cables: a knot or cable tie inside each hole, so a tug on a cable can't pull a joint apart.
  8. Run wires along the walls, not across the middle, and bundle them with a bit of tape. Label 24V and the R/G/B lines.
Simpler option: cut the barrel plug off the USB lead and bring it straight into the box through a knotted hole, wired to A/B. That drops the socket and one connection, and fewer connections means fewer dips. The trade-off is that the lead no longer unplugs from the box.
